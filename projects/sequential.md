# The Cognitive Basis of Sequential Effects

> **Level:** MSc

> **Keywords:** `sequential effects`, `decision making`, `cognitive modelling`, `response time`, `sequential sampling models`

---

## Description

Life is a series of choices. Normatively, each choice should be considered independently from all others, but human behaviour shows strong deviations from optimality: one choice can have a profound influence on the next. These carry-over effects are known as sequential effects.

In this project, we investigate the cognitive basis of sequential effects in a simple decision-making paradigm. What causes them? Is it repeated exposure to similar stimuli? Facilitatory effects on motor processing? Can we manipulate their strength — or perhaps mitigate them entirely?

The project combines experimental design and formal cognitive modelling to understand sequential effects and the cognitive mechanisms underlying decision making more broadly.

## Tasks

The student will be involved in:

## Data

Depending on the student's interest, the project can involve:
- **New data collection**: designing a new experiment and collecting data in the lab
- **Re-analysis of existing data**: developing new models and applying them to existing datasets

Experiments use simple perceptual decision-making paradigms. Primary dependent variables are **response times** and **choices**. No specific requirements for the participant sample.
